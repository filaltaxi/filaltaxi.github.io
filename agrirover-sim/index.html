    What the simulation caught

    Turning among plants

    Spinning round inside a lane brushes the canopy. The LiDAR caught it and stopped the rover, ending the mission.

    Fix: every turn happens in the headland past the row ends, and the rover lines up with the next row before it drives in.

    Drifting sideways

    By the third row the rover had drifted 17 cm towards the plants. Small heading errors add up, and GPS is far too coarse to notice.

    Fix: the LiDAR measures the distance to the row being scanned and corrects the position every tenth of a second.

    Distance on soil

    Without on-soil calibration, all 10 missions stopped at the first row end. Wheel slip made the rover think it was past the plants when it wasn't.

    Fix: drive 3 m on that field's soil at the start of each visit (bench test 7); slip differs field to field.

    What this does not prove

    • The leaf classifier is simulated: right about 90 % of the time in good light and 60 % for 1 plant in 5 shot in bad conditions. The real model's field accuracy is unknown until it is tested.
    • Soil slip (3 to 8 % by field), motor mismatch, IMU drift and GPS error are typical values, not measurements from your rover.
    • Plants are modelled as 15 cm circles. Real canopies are uneven and lean into lanes, so clearances in the field will be smaller.
    • Absolute positions stay about 1.5 m off: averaging 20 GPS fixes did not help, because the Neo-6M error drifts slowly. Positions along and across rows are good to a few centimetres.
    • The 20 % leaf rule was tuned on simulated leaves; retune it on your field test set.
